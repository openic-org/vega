## What the emulator is

`kuntur` `kuntur144/fpga/intan_rec_adapter/`, rewritten 2026-10-13 by
Manuel (Claude reviewed). Spec: `docs/interfaces/lvds-tunnel.md` §9.1a,
§9.1b.

- **Clocked by the controller's SCLK** (`spi0_sck`, `PCLKT3_2`, a primary
  clock input); CSb asynchronously resets the bit counter. No
  oversampling. The 12 MHz oscillator drives only the LED.
- **ROM only.** 64 × 8 `RHD_REG*` values from `rhd2164_defs.vh`, inferred
  into one EBR (`SP16K`); contents checked in the netlist `INITVAL`s.
  Updated only on `READ`; any other command's answer repeats the last
  `READ` value.
- **Two-word pipeline:** `data_rx` latches word N−1 at its 16th falling
  edge → ROM read at word N's 1st rising edge → `data_tx` at the 2nd
  rising edge → TX register at the 16th rising edge → shifted out in word
  N+1.
- MISO A during SCLK high, MISO B during SCLK low; all four streams carry
  the same data, so synthesis merged them into one register.

An earlier draft of the same day loaded the TX register asynchronously on
CSb falling, in a block with `rstb`, `sck` and `csb` all tested in the
`if` chain. That leaves no clock for synthesis to infer; it simulated
correctly but would not have built as intended. Fixed before this run.
The first synthesis of the rewrite also ran with the wrong top-level
module (`…_oversampling`, Radiant's own pick); corrected.

## What this settles

- **MOSI polarity.** An inverted `READ` (`11rrrrrr 00000000`) decodes as a
  `CONVERT`; the ROM would never have answered.
- **MISO1 and MISO2 polarity.** An inverted line returns the complement of
  `"INTAN"` and fails `getChipID()`. Both lines passed.
  With CS and SCLK (2026-10-10) and VDD orientation, **every signal on the
  C3430 harness is now verified**. The MOSI LED-check bitstream proposed
  on 10-10 is no longer needed.
- **Pipeline depth is exactly two words.** One word off shifts every
  answer one sample index and fails at all 16 delays (§9.1b).
- **SCLK clocking works at the controller's real SCLK.** §9.1a's design
  rule was argued from simulation; it now holds on hardware.

## What it does not settle

- **Delay margin.** RHX does not print the chosen MISO delay or how many
  of the 16 settings passed. Read the per-port cable delay from the
  Configure tab next session and record it as the baseline. A margin of
  one setting would pass today and fail on a different cable.
  *Afternoon: chosen delay read (5); margin deferred, see below.*
- **SCLK frequency.** Still the inferred 21 MHz (2800 × fs / 4), not
  measured. *Afternoon: measured 21.1 MHz, see below.*
- **Any waveform.** `CONVERT` answers are stale `READ` values, so the
  traces are meaningless. Nothing about data, the §9.5 frame switch, or
  `CONVERT(0)` as the sweep start is tested.
- **The testbench** still sends `READ(63)` on every word and could not
  have caught a wrong pipeline depth. RHX caught nothing because there was
  nothing to catch; the testbench should still be fixed before the next
  RTL change (§9.1b, consequence 1). *Fixed later the same day, see
  below.*

## Testbench: RHX command sequence

`tb.v` rewritten to replay what RHX actually sends (§9.1b), committed in
`kuntur` as `ba57867` (merge `c330952`); `qrun.f` changed `run 1us` →
`run -all` as `5eaa2f4` (merge `362e57c`), because `run 1us` stopped
after about one word.

- **Stimulus.** Behavioural SPI master at 21 MHz SCLK, 20 periods per
  word (16 data + CS high), MOSI changed after falling edges; MISO A
  sampled T/4 after each rising edge, MISO B T/4 after each falling edge.
  40 sample periods × 35 words (~1.33 ms): `CONVERT(0..31)`, then
  AuxCmd1 (`WRITE(3)`), AuxCmd2 (`CONVERT(32/33/34)`, `READ(40)`,
  `CONVERT(48)`), AuxCmd3 (the 60-command bank-0 register-config list
  with `CALIBRATE`), built as RHX builds them.
- **Check 1.** Every `READ` answered exactly two words later as
  `{8'h00, value}` on MISO A and B of both lines; a mismatch reports
  whether the answer matches depth 1 or 3 instead. Non-`READ` answers are
  counted, not checked (ROM-only: they repeat the last `READ`).
- **Check 2.** RHX `getChipID()` repeated on the AuxCmd3 results — index
  19 chip ID, 23 register 59, 24–26 "RHD", 32–36 "INTAN" — against
  literal values, not the `rhd2164_defs.vh` macros.

**Result (iverilog, `-g2012 -DSIM`, against the RTL in `54084a5`):**
`TB RESULT: PASS`, 32 `READ`s checked, detection passes on MISO1 and
MISO2. Register 59 on MISO B reads 53 where the spec wants 58; reported
as KNOWN OPEN, not a failure.

**Mutation check.** A scratch copy of the RTL with one extra word of

MISO2 passes, depth-3 diagnostic fires. The testbench now catches the

**Questa:** Manuel ran the new `tb.v` in Questa (`qrun.f`, `run -all`)

## Context — spec decisions, 2026-10-12/13

Merged as `3ec64ec` (`fc53b21`):

- **`TUN_CLK` forwards `ECLK` (42.5 MHz), not the ÷4 word clock.** The
  word clock (10.6 MHz) is below the LIFCL PLL's 18 MHz reference
  minimum, so the companion PLL in §6.6 could never have locked. Byte
  alignment is now found by preamble hunt + `IDDRX4.ALIGNWD` (new O2b).
- **Gearbox word clock renamed `WCLK`**, so "SCLK" means only the SPI
  clock.
- **§9.1b** documents what RHX sends, from RHX source `3c58677`.
- **§9.3's absorbed-write counter** would have counted ~30,000/s (RHX
  writes register 3 every sample period). Replaced by
  `host_write_changes` and the `host_mismatch_regs` bitmap.

**Non-`CONVERT` answers** (`kuntur` `b11df7d`, merge `bdbe1fd`). RTL by
Manuel; testbench by Claude. The ROM module became
`rhd2164_model_reg_ram`, and the answer is chosen from the decoded
command at the existing `tx_load` edge, so the pipeline is unchanged:

| Command | Answer |
|---|---|
| `READ(R)` | `{0x00, value}`; register 59 = `0x35` on MISO A, **`0x3A` on MISO B** |
| `WRITE(R,D)` | `{0xFF, D}`; **absorbed, not applied** (§9.3) |
| `CALIBRATE`, `CLEAR` | `0x0000` (`RHD_TWOSCMP = 1`) |
| `CONVERT` | unchanged: repeats the last answer |

The first version applied writes to the register memory. Claude's
review caught it against §9.3: a later `READ` must return Kuntur's value,
not the host's. Manuel commented the write port out. **The testbench had
passed that version**, because it wrote Kuntur's own values. It now
writes `reg ^ 0x5A`, so an applied write shows up on the next
`READ(0..17)`.

Testbench changes: every non-`CONVERT` answer is checked two words
later on all four MISO streams. `N_SAMPLES` went from 40 to 60, since at
40 `CALIBRATE` (AuxCmd3 entry 54) and `WRITE 18–21` were never sent; the
tb now fails if no `CALIBRATE` is checked. The depth diagnostic fires
only on `READ` failures, since stale answers matched neighbouring
`READ`s by coincidence.

**Synthesis** (Manuel, `.srp` 11:52): top `intan_rec_adapter`. The
register memory now maps to LUTs (94 LUT4, 13 WIDEFN9, no EBR), since it
has two read outputs. Harmless. Bitstream rebuilt but **not committed

**Deferred:** `host_write_changes` / `host_mismatch_regs` (§9.3). Nothing
can read them before O8, so synthesis would remove them, and the bitmap
needs `CONFIG` from the tunnel.

**Spec, `lvds-tunnel.md` §9.2** (vega `952494e`). RHX expects offset
binary: it writes `twosComp = 0` and computes µV as `0.195 × (v − 32768)`
(RHX `3c58677`, `rhxregisters.cpp:188`, `waveformfifo.cpp:337`). Kuntur
runs two's complement. The companion inverts bit 15 of `CONVERT(0..31)`
answers, and the tunnel stays raw. The underrun sentinel `0x8000` must
be inserted **before** that XOR. After it, `0x8000` is mid-scale and a
dead link would show as a quiet recording (§7.3).

## Bench, 14:30–14:46: new bitstream, delay, SCLK, MOSI

**Bitstream loaded:** `impl_1/intan_rec_adapter_impl_1.bit`, built

**RHX detects 128 channels again.** Start-up output is identical to the
morning's (FrontPanel 5.3.4, serial `2545001ING`, Rhythm version 1, no
`Error I…`). The one new line, `qt.multimedia.ffmpeg: Using Qt multimedia
with FFmpeg 5.1.4`, is Qt's audio back-end and unrelated to SPI. This is
the first hardware run of the new `WRITE`/`CALIBRATE`/register 59
answers. Detection does not read those answers, so it shows only that
they broke nothing.

**Port A cable delay: 5** (Configure tab → Manual → the Port A spin box,
which shows RHX's chosen value; cancelled without change). This is the
baseline for this bitstream and C3430 harness. **The margin is still
unknown.** RHX never reports how many of the 16 settings passed. Its
rule (`rhxcontroller.cpp:1870–1897`) picks the only one, the longer of
two for an RHD2164, or the second-shortest of three or more, so one
value cannot tell a single passing setting from a wide window.
Measuring it would need a debug print in a rebuilt RHX, or a scope
measurement. **Deferred by Manuel until after phases A and B.**

**SCLK: 21.1 MHz** measured, against the inferred 21 MHz. **CS period:
960 ns**, periodic, **with no gap between sample periods**. 35 words ×
952 ns (80 clocks of 84 MHz) is exactly the 33.3 µs period, so nothing
is left over for a gap. The 960 vs 952 ns is cursor accuracy. So a frame
boundary can only come from decoding commands (`CONVERT(0)`), never from
CS timing. That backs §9.2 and §9.5.

**MOSI capture** (Keysight DSOX1204G, ch1, one leg of the LVDS pair
through a 10:1 probe, 500 mV/div, 4.1 µs/div, 520 mV pk-pk, photo 14:46).
There is one silent word every ~8.2 div ≈ 34 µs, i.e. once per sample
period. Manuel counted 34 non-zero words plus that one. `CONVERT(0)` =
`0x0000` is the only all-zero word in RHX's sequence. The densest words
sit just before each silent word and sparse single spikes follow, which
is the pattern of aux (`WRITE(3)`, aux2, `READ(63)` = `0xFF00`) →
`CONVERT(0)` → `CONVERT(1..31)`. **Consistent with the §9.1b slot order
and with `CONVERT(0)` opening each sweep, but not decoded.** At
4.1 µs/div one bit is about one pixel. A ~200 ns/div capture across the
silent word would settle it before §9.5 relies on it. The scope's
`Freq(1) = 1.049 MHz` reading is the counter locking onto pulse edges;
ignore it.

### MOSI decoded, 14:54

Manuel zoomed into the two silent words of the same recording (photos
IMG_1696, 760 ns/div, delay −11.07 µs; IMG_1697, 980 ns/div, delay
+21.58 µs; both 14:54). Words were placed by the timebase (952 ns per
word, 47 ns per bit) and pulses read by their offset from the word
start (bit 15 first; `CONVERT(c)` has its ones in bits 13..8):

| Word | IMG_1696 | IMG_1697 | Decodes as |
|---|---|---|---|
| … | ~5 bits, from bit 13 | pulses of ~4 and ~5 bits | `CONVERT(29..31)` |
| aux1 | narrow at start + short pulse ~6 bits in | same | `WRITE(3, D)`: bit 15, bits 9–8 |
| aux2 | ~5 bits at start | single spike 2 bits in | `READ(40)` / `CONVERT(32)` |
| aux3 | **8 bits at start** | **8 bits at start** | **`READ(63)` = `0xFF00`** |
| next | **silent** | **silent** | **`CONVERT(0)`** |
| next 3 | single spikes stepping bit 8 → 9 → 9–8 | same | `CONVERT(1)`, `(2)`, `(3)` |

**The §9.1b slot order is confirmed at word level.** It runs
`CONVERT(0..31)`, aux1, aux2, aux3, and `CONVERT(0)` opens each sweep.
The two captures are from different sample periods: aux2 differs, as
AuxCmd2 cycles 32/33/34/40, which also shows the aux lists advancing.
The photos limit bit positions to about ±1 bit. The decode rests on the
order of four distinctive words (8-bit block, silent word, single
spikes stepping by one bit), which no other slot order produces. The
testbench's sequence therefore matches the wire. With no CS gap,
`CONVERT(0)` is the only frame marker §9.5 has.
