## 1. The drop counter under-reports burst loss, silently and downward

**This is the headline, and it invalidates how "Dropped" has been read all
along.**

Throughput was flat at a steady-state median of **500.00 pkt/s**, but the
run's mean was **499.35 pkt/s**. Small — and multiplied by 62,131 s it is
**~40,400 packets missing** (0.13%, ~2.39 M samples, ~81 s of data). The
panel reports 727. Those are not the same measurement.

`pc-app/serial_reader.py:263`:

```python
gap = (seq - self._expected_seq) % 256
```

`seq_num` is a rolling byte, so **every gap is taken mod 256**. The single
33-second stall below lost ~7,220 packets in one burst; it contributed
`7220 % 256` = **52** to that 727. A burst of exactly 256×k packets
reports as **zero**.

So `dropped_packets` is not a loss count. It is a sum of per-event losses
*modulo 256* — a lower bound that collapses precisely in the bursty regime
that matters, and fails in the dangerous direction: **a worse night reads
as a cleaner number.** 727 out of 31 M looks like an excellent link. It is
an artifact of the loss having been bursty.

**A byte counter cannot carry this.** There is no arithmetic fix inside
`serial_reader.py`. The honest options are:

1. **Flag, don't guess.** When a gap is ≥ some threshold, record
   "gap detected, true size unknown (≥ N)" and stop pretending the sum is
   a total. Cheap, correct, and immediately stops the panel lying.
2. **Get `0xFFF4` working** — the MCU's own counters are cumulative and
   32-bit, and do not wrap at a packet boundary. See §3.

Option 1 is a stopgap for the number on screen; option 2 is the real
answer, and this run is the strongest argument yet for it.

### Caveat on the 40,400

That figure depends on 500.00 pkt/s being the loss-free rate. Measured
against the p90 (500.80) the deficit is ~90,100; if the transport genuinely
paces at 499.35 it is near zero. **The bounded, independently confirmed
part is the 33-second hole** — visible in the bench gap *and* in the system
journal — and that one event alone is 10× the entire reported drop count.
The mechanism (mod-256 truncation) is certain; the total is a range.

## 2. The one real hole was the host, not the link — 04:36:18 to 04:36:51

| | |
|---|---|
| Gap in bench rows | 33.1 s (t = 38,779.6 → 38,812.7) |
| Packets in window | 9,334 vs ~16,550 expected |
| Lost | ~7,220 pkt ≈ 425,700 samples ≈ **14.4 s of data** |

Cause, from `journalctl`, at the far end of that window:

```
04:36:52 systemd-oomd: Killed .../snap.firefox... due to memory pressure
         for /user.slice/user-1000.slice/user@1000.service
         being 84.90% > 50.00% for > 20s with reclaim activity
04:36:52 kernel: workqueue: delayed_fput hogged CPU for >10000us 19 times
```

The workstation was thrashing (swap still 100% full — 4.0 Gi used, 35 Mi
free — when checked at 11:05). The whole `main.py` process was starved for
~30 s: the Qt status timer missed 8 ticks and the reader thread stalled
long enough that the kernel tty buffer (33 s × ~122 KB/s ≈ 4 MB) overflowed.

**Nothing to fix in the firmware, the bridge, or the radio.** But it is a
real finding for the animal recording: *desktop memory pressure can eat 14
seconds of a one-shot capture*, and the only reason we know it happened is
that the bench log has a hole in it. Had this occurred during a recording,
the CSV would have a 14-second discontinuity and the panel would have shown
+52 drops.

**No link-level event.** `journalctl -k` shows no USB event on the bridge
port (`ttyACM1`, STLINK-V3 `003B0026…`) after the 17:49 plug-in. The one
USB reset overnight (07:02:32, `usb 1-6`) is the Goodix fingerprint reader;
throughput across that moment is flat at 500.1 pkt/s.

**43 smaller dips**, each a single 2–4 s interval at ~470 pkt/s (50–120
packets, ~0.1–0.2 s of data). Scattered, no periodicity — spacing 60 s to
5,516 s. Unattributed; see §3 for why they must stay that way.

## 3. Telemetry is dead on hardware — A.7 step 2 has now failed its bench test

**`Telemetry: no frames` after 17 continuous hours.** The `0xFFF4` plane
did not produce a single frame. CLAUDE.md and PLAN.md both carry A.7 step 2
as "code complete, desk-verified, awaiting bench bring-up". This run *was*
the bench bring-up, unintentionally, and it is a **negative result**: 17 h
against a streaming headstage is not an ambiguous test.

The suspect is the one named in the spec as the fragile part — the bridge's
connection sequence now writes a **fourth CCCD**, and nothing desk-side can
exercise it. Next session should check, in order: does the MCU notify on
`0xFFF4` at all (is `STREAM_APP` assembling frames?), does the bridge
discover the characteristic, does the fourth CCCD write succeed, does
`0xDD 0x22` reach the host.

**The cost of it being dead is exactly this write-up.** All five attribution
fields read `—`, so the ~40,400 missing packets and the 43 dips cannot be
split between radio loss, MCU ring truncation, and bridge USB backlog. The
subsystem built to answer "was it the air or the host?" was the subsystem
not reporting, which is why §1 had to infer loss from a rate curve and could
only produce a range.

## 4. Underruns 0% — inconsistent with the documented A.7 retune

Panel says **0 underruns (0.0%)**, and 499.8 pkt/s × 59 = **29,488 SPS**,
~1.7% under the 30,000 nominal.

CLAUDE.md (§ channel liveness) and PLAN.md A.1.2 both state that after the
A.7 PLL retune **~5% of samples are `0x8000` underrun padding by design**
(λ < μ) — a claim load-bearing enough that `channel_health.py` excludes
underrun samples specifically so the padding would not reset the dead-run
counter forever.

Zero padding alongside a rate deficit is not what that describes. Either
the retune is not in this build, or the ~5% figure is stale. **Unresolved
— do not edit the docs on the strength of one panel reading.** Check
`STREAM_ACTIVE_MODE`, the shipped λ, and whether `0x8000` padding is emitted
at all in this bitstream before touching the 5% claim anywhere.

Note the coupling: if padding is genuinely absent, the exclusion rule in
`channel_health.py` is currently inert — harmless, but it means the rule has
never actually been exercised on hardware.
