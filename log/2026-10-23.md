## Receiver tasks 1–3: Manuel writes them

Manuel writes the `cdr_fsm` fixes, the aligner outputs and the checker.
The aligner outputs (`dout`, `pos`) and the checker are specified in
`docs/interfaces/lvds-tunnel.md` §6.6. Claude's part: wire the checker
into `cdr_tb` beside `rx_ref` and compare `no_lock` and `errors` at all
280 steps.

`cdr_fsm` findings, from reading the code (not simulated):

- `data_is_aligned` is undriven; it should come from `aligner_valid`.
- `aligner_rst` has no default, so it latches 1 after the first
  `aligner_rdy`; the aligner is then held in reset and the FSM hangs
  in `op0`.
- The FSM runs all 8 `bitsel`s. The last `bitsel_en` wraps 7 → 0 and
  the next reset clears `bitsel_aligned`, so the result is lost. Fix:
  stop at the first match and leave `bitsel` there.
- `cdr0.lock` is wired to `pll_lock` in the top level, but `cdr_fsm`
  never reads it. PLAN.md said "undeclared"; that is out of date.
- For the sweep controller, later: `phase_en`, `phasestep` and
  `eye_edge_en` are hardwired in `cdr_fsm`, so `phase` counts every
  `wclk`.

## Tasks 1–2 done; `cdr_tb` extended

Manuel's first version of the `cdr_fsm` fixes and aligner outputs. In the
`cdr_tb` updated for them (Icarus, `+n=20`), two checks failed at every
step:

- **`cdr` never reached `rdy`.** Nothing reset `frame_aligner` when `cdr`
  started: `aligner_rst` only fired after the aligner had reported `rdy`.
  In simulation `cnt0` started as X, so the search never timed out. On
  hardware, power-up initialisation would hide the first hunt, and every
  later restart (phase step, relock) would accept the previous step's
  match at once. **Fix A:** pulse `aligner_rst` in `idle`.
- **`pos` was one byte behind `dout`** (`pos` = 3 with a `cnt` byte on
  `dout`). The edge that loads `pos` also moves `cnt[7:0]` into
  `data_sa_reg_0`. **Fix B:** seed `pos` at 4.

Both were confirmed on a scratch copy of the RTL first, then applied by
Manuel together with `idle` waiting for `lock`. Result: ALL PASS. The
aligner's `bitsel`, `dout`/`pos` on every fixed byte, and `cdr` whole all
agree with `rx_ref` at all 256 error-free steps. `+q7first` fails as
intended.

`cdr_tb.sv` changes (Claude):

- uses the new aligner ports;
- checks `dout` against `pos` for 16 bytes after each match, at
  error-free steps only (eye-edge steps carry real bit errors);
- instantiates `cdr` whole, with `lock` = 1, resets it at every step and
  checks its final `bitsel`.
