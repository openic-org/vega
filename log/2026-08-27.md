## 2. Chip0 placement, round 2 — MOSI/SCK, not MISO

Bench session started by reflashing the 2026-08-26 regbank
EBR/RTL-reorg/parametrization changes, which had never been verified on
hardware. Round-trip worked, but real-channel data showed chip0 dead
again (`0xFFFF` flat on friendly channel 42) — same signature as the
2026-08-24 chip0-placement bug. Chased it down the MISO path first
(pad-distance analysis, an asymmetric extra `LUT4` in `rxsr_a0`/
`rxsr_b0`) before Manuel corrected the diagnosis: the actual broken
signals were MOSI/SCK. That MISO analysis is recorded in PLAN.md as a
documented dead end, not deleted, since the underlying facts (pad
distances, the LUT asymmetry) are still true, just not causal here.

Root cause, worked out together: `spi1_mosi`/`spi1_sck`/`spi1_csb` are
shared — one FPGA pad each, fanning out on the PCB to *both* RHD2164
chips. FPGA-internal placement can't differentially compensate chip0 vs
chip1 for a shared signal; what it *can* do is minimize the
FPGA-internal share of the fixed total period, leaving more margin for
the external (PCB, chip0-vs-chip1-asymmetric, and — confirmed with
Manuel — unmeasurable with the oscilloscope on hand, wrong bandwidth
and no pad access) share to absorb. Tight placement has been standing
in for an honest bound on that external delay the whole time.

Found and fixed a second, independent problem along the way: the
placement-pinning approach tried mid-session (`ldc_create_group`
enumerating every FSM leaf cell by its synthesis-auto-generated name)
silently dropped one cell — `i6_1_lut` didn't resolve
(`WARNING <1026001>`, no build failure) because that name isn't stable
across resynthesis. Fixed by reverting to the 2026-08-26 "item-4"
whole-design scheme: `ldc_set_location -region <name> [get_cells
<RTL-instance-name>]` for every major block, which references RTL
names instead of synthesis internals.

**Verified, all four legs, on real hardware:** placement (spi1_rhd2164x2's
full sub-instance set landed together, R21-24 x C42-47, inside the
declared region), timing (0 violations, all 3 corners, 98.2% coverage),
area (SLICE/LUT down from the 2026-08-26 baseline, matching A.1.4's
dead-port cleanup landing in the same pass — confirmed the `DPR16X4`
residual noted in every area report since 2026-08-26 is gone), and a
full `SET_CHANNELS`/readback round trip with both chips streaming real
data. Committed `kuntur` `e89671d`, pushed and merged to `main`.

## 3. Real signal validation

With the placement fix confirmed, injected an 8 mVpp/1 kHz signal from
a generator and read it back through the real RTL/ADC/µV-conversion
chain: measured 8.294 mV peak-to-peak (3.7% off, using the same
0.195 µV/LSB gain A.6.2 landed yesterday) and a signal frequency
consistent with the actual (not nominal) sample rate once the
wall-clock-vs-synthetic-timestamp distinction was sorted out (worth
remembering: `packet_parser.py`'s per-sample timestamps are
*synthesized* from a hardcoded constant, not measured — don't compute
"real" SPS from them again).

After removing the generator and scope, both channels showed a clean,
near-exact 60.00 Hz line dominating the spectrum (~3 mV amplitude, with
120/180/300 Hz harmonics) — floating, unshielded inputs picking up
mains hum, exactly as Manuel predicted before the check.
`pc-app/analysis/spectrum_20260827_ch0_ch1.png`.

## 4. PLL retune — exact, but it moved the bottleneck

Cycle-counted the actual FPGA sampling rate from RTL (46 `clk`
cycles/slot x 33 slots/frame): the design was running ~29,348 SPS, not
the nominal 30,000 it was supposedly built for — confirmed against
Manuel's own oscilloscope measurement of the `spi1_csb` period (1.03 us
measured vs. 1.0326 us computed, 0.25% agreement) before touching
anything. Required `clk` for exactly 30,000 SPS: 45.54 MHz. Manuel
retuned the PLL; actual `CLKOP_FREQ_ACTUAL` landed at 45.539955 MHz —
29,999.97 SPS, essentially exact.

One thing caught in the process: the retune didn't initially update
`impl_1.sdc`'s `clk` constraint, so STA was still checking against the
old, slower period. Fixed (`create_clock ... -period 21.9587393093`).
Re-ran STA clean, but the worst `spi1_sck` margin tightened to
**0.915 ns** — still passing, but the tightest margin recorded across
this whole investigation, and a real, expected cost of the faster
clock: the same external PCB/chip delays now eat a bigger fraction of
a shorter period.

**Then the real finding.** With production now genuinely at ~30,000
SPS, measured *delivered* rate over a 10 s live capture came in at
29,482.9 SPS — 1.7% short of the new target, whereas before the retune
delivery comfortably matched or exceeded the (lower) production rate.
Underrun measured 0%, consistent with production now *exceeding*
delivery rather than the reverse — the FPGA's 4096-pair ring buffer
would fill (not drain) at the implied ~517 pair/s deficit, in about 8 s,
inside the capture window, and excess samples are most likely being
silently dropped on the write side, which doesn't trip the read-side
`0x8000` underrun sentinel at all. A different failure shape than the
one the 2026-08-03 mblock-margin fix targeted, and it now has real
margin room removed by the tighter `spi1_sck` timing on top.

**Not root-caused or fixed today — deliberately held for tomorrow.**
