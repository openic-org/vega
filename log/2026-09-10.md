# 2026-09-10 — chip0 reproduced on a cold start, and the experiment that would have missed it

First cold start since the liveness detector existed. **The fault
reproduced, the instrument caught the whole cycle, and the results
invalidate the experiment that was queued up next.**

## The session

Powered off overnight (~18.5 h), reflashed, connected 11:17:42.
Thermostat 70 °F, outside 75 °F. Ch A = 42 / Ch B = 94 confirmed on the
panel. Both channels live at connect.

`pc-app/bench/health_20260910_111742.csv`:

```
16:17:42Z  ch0  42  alive   (connect)
16:29:46Z  ch0  42  dead    prior alive  724.259 s
16:49:21Z  ch0  42  alive   prior dead  1174.585 s
16:49:22Z  ch0  42  dead    prior alive    1.390 s
17:05:49Z  ch0  42  alive   prior dead   986.732 s
```

Onset **12 min 04 s** after connect. Dead **36 min 01 s** total.
Spontaneous recovery, nothing touched at any edge. Ch B normal throughout
and throughput flat at ~500 pkt/s across every transition, so none of it is
a link artifact. Stable for 31 min after recovery at time of writing.

Full analysis in `log/chip0-temperature-trials.md` § Trial 6. Three
findings, in order of how much they cost us.

## 1. The queued pass-rate experiment would have concluded the opposite of the truth

PLAN.md A.1.2 item 0 specified **ten power cycles at ~2 minutes each** —
off, on, reflash, check — as the cheapest-decisive next step, and
everything else in A.1.2 was gated on its result.

**Trial 6's fault onset at 12 min 04 s.** A two-minute check scores it a
**pass**: the channel was live and completely normal for the whole window.
Run as written, the experiment manufactures false passes, reports a
near-zero failure rate — and near-zero is precisely the result that
retires the thermal hypotheses and cancels every item below it.

The design is now marked invalid in the plan. Minimum per-cycle
observation is the longest onset delay ever seen plus margin — **20
minutes**, putting ten cycles at 3.5+ hours instead of under an hour. Also
added: **score from `health_*.csv`, never by eye** (see §2), and a note
that fewer, longer-watched cycles may buy more than ten shallow ones, since
the quantity in doubt is no longer just *whether* a boot fails but *when*.

**The near-miss is the lesson.** The 2-minute figure came from trial 4's
~2 min onset — one observation, treated as a constant. It was about to be
built into an experiment whose null result would have closed the
investigation.

## 2. A 1.39-second recovery, invisible to every previous method

At 16:49:21Z chip0 drove the line for **1.39 seconds**, then failed again
for another 16 minutes.

**A thermal process cannot do this.** A board warming through a threshold
does not cross it, hold 1.4 seconds, and cross back. This is the signature
of a **marginal timing edge** — the SPI link close enough to failure that
something fast decides the outcome — not a slow drift.

It is also unobservable by the method used for trials 0–4, which was
looking at a screen. A 1.4-second event between two 16-minute dead
stretches would never have been seen. **If the fault does this routinely,
the earlier trials were sampling a signal they could not resolve.**

**Weight, stated honestly:** `channel_health.py` uses
`ALIVE_ENTER_SAMPLES = 16` and `DEAD_ENTER_SEC = 0.25`, so the alive edge
needed 16 consecutive non-`0xFFFF` samples (~0.5 ms at 29.5 kSPS). More
than one corrupted packet can fake, but not a large margin. **Open
question, answerable at the desk:** does 16 samples prove chip0 was
genuinely driving MISO, or could a partial/garbled SPI read clear that bar?
Settle it before treating this as established mechanism.

## 3. Temperature: two failures on a variable never measured in the room

Trial 6 is the **first FAIL with any ambient recorded at all**, and cold
start after an overnight power-off is now **2 for 2 on failure** (0b, 6).
The one cold pass that disagrees — trial 3, "checked promptly" — is
undermined by §1: promptly may simply have meant *before onset*.

But an overstatement of mine got caught and corrected the same session.
I had written trial 6 as "the coldest condition in the series". **It is
not established as that.** The thermostat sets the temperature **one floor
above the test room**, single zone, no known offset — lowering the setpoint
2 °F changes what the *upper* floor is held at, and the test room is
coupled to that but not controlled by it. Corrected in both docs to
"lowest setpoint in the series; test room probably but not verifiably
colder".

**That correction exposed a circularity that had been standing since
2026-09-05.** The ~$30 thermo-hygrometer was deferred on the reasoning
"trials 2–3 showed the room reading barely moves" — but that reading came
from a thermostat controlling a different floor. It was never a
measurement of the room the board is in, so it could not have shown what it
was cited for. **The premise for deferring the purchase does not hold**, and
the item is now un-gated in the plan: order it *before* the next pass-rate
attempt, which now costs 3.5+ hours and would otherwise run blind.

## 4. Bitstream verified

Closed a gap that had been open across two trials:

```
$ sha256sum fpga/kuntur_fpga/impl_1/kuntur_fpga_impl_1.bit
cdc7d39dca801aa8864cb0840d6aac1d2d8601c34c5026c80a1dd72d90da9fa7
```

Matches the pinned value; mtime 2026-09-04 15:16 (untouched six days);
tree clean; last modified by `761d662`, an ancestor of `kuntur` HEAD. No
rebuild slipped in. Trial 6 records it as verified, trial 5 by inference
(no flash occurred for trial 5 — the SRAM configuration came from an
earlier, unrecorded flash).

**Limit:** this hashes the file on disk, not the configuration in the part.
The FPGA is SRAM-configured with no readback, so it guarantees "the file
flashed from was the pinned one" and only for a flash from that path.
Routine from here: `sha256sum` after every flash, first eight characters
into the trial row.

## 5. Thermal kit: specified and ordered

Approval this morning was for a four-line cost table. Turning it into part
numbers changed three of the four lines and removed one entirely.

### The meter line went to $0 — the bench already had a better instrument

The kit budgeted "$15–30, dual-channel K-type meter". The **Keysight
U1242B already on the bench is a two-channel K thermometer**: separate
`+T1/−T1` and `+T2/−T2` inputs, displaying **T1, T2 and `T1−T2`** plus a
scan mode, −40 °C to 1000 °C.

`T1−T2` **is** the localisation measurement. Chip0 on T1, neighbour on T2,
read the differential live while spraying — which is exactly the
comparison that separates "the board got cold and something broke" (known)
from "*this part* got cold" (the actual goal of step 3).

The jack mapping is not printed on the panel and mattered, because if T1
and T2 shared COM two banana adapters could not both plug in. They don't:
**T1 = V/Ω + COM, T2 = µA/mA + A.** Confirmed indirectly but decisively
from the manual's *Input-A Warning Alert*, which says the "lead in the A
jack" alarm is suppressed "in T1/T2 temperature measurements mode" — i.e.
that jack legitimately carries the second thermocouple.

The **DMM6500** takes the third channel and covers the U1242B's one
weakness, a −40 °C floor against a −51 °C spray. Two ways: native TC mode
reaches −200 °C (reference junction is *simulated* at the front terminals;
internal CJC needs a 2001-TCSCAN card), or read the raw thermocouple
**voltage** in DCV — no floor, 6½ digits, and **no CJC needed at all for a
delta** at ~41 µV/°C. Every question in A.1.2 is a delta. It is also the
logging channel: deep timestamped buffer, so a thermal trace aligns
against `bench/health_*.csv` on one timebase. Trial 6's lesson was that
the transition log is the instrument, not a human watching a screen; this
extends that to temperature.

### The dew-point precondition was unachievable as written

PLAN.md said: know the dew point, "stop if the board approaches it." The
chosen spray is **MG Chemicals 403A, 100 % HFC-134a, −51 °C**. Every
trigger pull puts the target hundreds of degrees below any room dew point,
instantly. **Condensation is not a risk to be held away from, it is a
certainty to be budgeted.**

The hazard is unchanged — water on the board creates leakage paths that
can cause or mask faults, and the failure mode is chasing a condensation
artifact and recording it as chip0. What changed is the mitigation: short
bursts with **full rewarm and dry between**, never stacking a burst onto
an already-cold target; **visible frost as the stop signal**, since there
is no threshold to stay above; ambient RH as the only real lever.

The hygrometer's justification changed with it. It is no longer a go/no-go
gate — there is no gate to hold — but it keeps the job that always
mattered more: **the ambient record every trial in this series has
lacked.**

### The purpose-built surface probe was the wrong part, and was nearly rejected for the wrong reason

The Omega **SA1-K-SC** self-adhesive surface thermocouple is the obvious
part for "measure a chip's surface temperature". It is disqualified here by
a **19 × 25 mm pad** — larger than the RHD2164 package, overhanging onto
neighbours and bare PCB. It would give an excellent reading of the wrong
quantity, averaging across precisely the boundary step 3 exists to
resolve. Its adhesive is also documented as "not for repetitive
reapplication", and the experiment is inherently repetitive.

**The near-miss:** DigiKey lists it 0 °C to 175 °C, which reads as an
instant disqualification at −51 °C. Omega's own manual gives the pad an
actual rating of **−60 to 177 °C**. It would have been rejected on a
catalogue figure that is not a physical limit — the same shape of error as
the Omega 5TC (listed 0 °C) and, in the other direction, the thermostat
proxy. **A distributor's range field is not a spec.**

Chosen instead: **Labfacility XE-3506-001** — exposed welded junction,
1/0.2 mm (32 AWG), −75 °C, Class 1, miniature plug, sold individually. The
bare junction matters because a sheathed or laminated probe's thermal mass
under-reads a 1–2 s burst; fine gauge matters because fat wire thermally
shorts the package to ambient and reads the cold gas stream. Kapton tape
over the bead recovers the one genuine advantage the SA1 pad had —
shielding the junction from the gas — at ~1 mm of footprint.

### One hygrometer, not two

First recommendation was two, one at the board and one at room level. That
was wrong: **dew point is the well-mixed quantity, temperature is not.**
Absolute humidity equalises across a closed room, so one sensor anywhere
reasonable gives the dew point, which is the number the frost budget
needs. Temperature at the board is separately covered by a thermocouple in
free air near the PCB — better than a hygrometer would do it.

Chosen: **Govee H5075**, and its broadcast format is documented, so the
`bench/ambient_*.csv` logger needs no reverse-engineering. Decode recorded
in PLAN.md. One trap worth the ink: **it requires an ACTIVE scan** — the
payload rides in the scan response. `bleak` scans actively by default so
this is free, but a passive-only setup sees nothing and presents exactly
like a dead sensor.

### Open

- **`bench/ambient_*.csv` has no interface spec yet**, and the project
  convention is that one precedes the code. Needs: columns, the timestamp
  basis that makes it joinable with `health_*.csv`, the dew-point formula,
  and how an error-flagged or stale sample is represented. The H5075's
  battery byte carries an **error flag in bit 7** — a sensor reporting an
  error must not silently become an ambient record.
- **Unverified: does the Linux box have a BLE adapter?** The pc-app path
  is USB serial to the WB09KE bridge, so host BLE has never been needed. A
  ~$10 dongle covers it, but finding out on 09-11 wastes the head start.
- **Test-fit the probes on arrival.** That DigiKey line is
  **non-cancelable/non-returnable**, and two things are unconfirmed: that
  the miniature plug mates with the Klein socket, and that two 38 mm
  adapter bodies clear each other across 19 mm jack spacing.
