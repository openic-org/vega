# 2026-08-24 — Session Log

## Summary

Two problems closed today, one of which turned out to be much bigger than
the question that opened it. **The FPGA had never been timing-constrained**
— `impl_1.sdc` had no real `create_clock` at all — and fixing that
surfaced a genuine setup violation on the RHD2164 MISO capture path, fixed
by pipelining two FSM outputs. Verifying that fix on hardware then
revealed chip0 had been dead the whole time (first noted 2026-08-11), and
what started as "check the timing fix" turned into a full bring-up
investigation that ended in **unconstrained FPGA placement** as the real
root cause — nothing to do with timing margins, PCB signal integrity, or
logic content, all of which were tested and ruled out first.

Three findings outweigh the checklist:

- **The design was completely unconstrained**, silently. No `create_clock`
  anywhere in `impl_1.sdc`; every SPI1 path (and everything else) closed
  on whatever the router happened to do.
- **Chip0's non-responsiveness was a placement bug, not a signal-integrity
  or logic bug.** `spi1_rhd2164x2` had no physical location constraint and
  drifted to a worse spot as the design grew across builds. Pinning it
  (and `controller0`) with `ldc_create_region`/`ldc_set_location` fixed it
  — confirmed by restoring the *entire* original design one block at a
  time with placement held fixed, real channel data flowing from both
  chips throughout.
- **The placement fix's side effect on CSB.** Re-running the full,
  accurate constraint set on the placement-pinned design showed MISO/MOSI
  margins *improved*, but CSB's margin against the (unmeasured, estimated)
  1.5 ns board/cable budget narrowed from +1.563 ns to +1.462 ns. Small,
  still passing, worth tracking.

## 1. FPGA timing constraints — from zero to a real violation to a fix

Raised 2026-08-11 as an open question, started today. `impl_1.pdc` had one
stray `create_clock -period 10` (100 MHz, wrong) and nothing else — no
`clk`, no `spi1_sck`, no MISO/MOSI/CSB delays. Built the full set from
scratch, split correctly this time between Radiant's two constraint files
once Manuel pointed out the split (`impl_1.sdc` = pre-synthesis timing,
`impl_1.pdc` = post-synthesis physical — confirmed via `kuntur_fpga.rdf`'s
own file-type tags):

- `clkin` at the real 31.25 ns (32 MHz), `clk` as a generated clock off
  the PLL (`-multiply_by 71 -divide_by 51` → 44.55 MHz), `spi1_sck` as
  `clk`'s divide-by-2.
- MISO input delay, MOSI/CSB output delay, derived from the RHD2000 series
  datasheet's SPI timing table (§3 correction: `tMOSI` is 10 ns flat, not
  the 10.4 ns Manuel recalled — verified by reading the actual datasheet
  page image, not just `pdftotext`, which mis-tables it).
- A `set_multicycle_path` exception for the `clk`→`spi1_sck` divide-by-2
  crossing, needed after Radiant's syntax rejected the first two attempts
  (FPGA-AN-02059 §3.3.4: no bare trailing object allowed).

First STA run found a real setup violation on the RHD2164 MISO capture
path (`a0`/`b0` in `sr_s2p`), traced to `spi_master_controller`'s
`rx_a_en`/`rx_b_en` being purely combinational. Manuel explicitly rejected
slowing `sck` to fix it ("I don't want to reduce data rate") and pipelined
the enables instead — moved their combinational assertion one FSM state
earlier so the registered output lands on the same state-relative edge as
before. Fixed MISO, but tightened `spi1_csb`'s own margin to near-zero as
a side effect; pipelined `csb` itself the same way as a follow-up. Result:
all four SPI1 signals clean, 0 setup/hold errors, all corners
(`fpga-timing-constraints.md` §5.5). Manuel's own simulation run confirmed
the pipelining didn't shift A.1.1's `SLOT_OFFSET = 3` slot-to-channel
mapping.

Committed to `kuntur` as `c439c21`.

## 2. Verifying the fix in hardware found a different, older bug

Manuel's real goal in verifying the fix: "the main reason to do the fix is
to bring chip0 alive." Chip0 had been silently dead since first noticed
2026-08-11, and stayed dead after the timing fix. What followed was a
full investigation, hypothesis by hypothesis (full detail, including the
ruled-out ones, in the new spec — see below):

1. **PCB trace-length skew** (2.5 mm difference, chip1's branch longer) —
   real topology finding via `kuntur144-nil.kicad_pcb`, but the magnitude
   (~15–20 ps) is three orders of magnitude too small to explain total
   non-responsiveness.
2. **`TIMING_MARGIN_PCT` scaling** — a 100% setting did produce an STA
   violation, but it's a structural artifact of the SDC (fixed external
   RHD delay budgets don't scale with the margin knob), not a real signal.
3. **`DELAYA`/`DELAYB` output-delay primitives** — researched as a
   targeted fix, found and documented (not in the more obvious sysIO User
   Guide), never needed.
4. **Clock speed** — decisive negative result. Ran the design at 32 MHz
   (PLL bypassed) and again at 10 MHz (PLL reconfigured); chip0 stayed
   dead at both, ruling out any timing-margin or signal-settling
   explanation and redirecting the investigation toward "something
   functional changed."
5. **Logic content** — Manuel stripped `kuntur_fpga.v` to a bare minimum
   (PLL bypass, `start` tied high, most modules removed). Both chips
   responded. Traced two specific candidates from there —
   `rhd2164_controller`'s pacing gap (RTL-traced: the full/paced design
   actually gives *more* recovery time than the stripped version, ruling
   it out) and `RHD_CALIBRATE` config-table content (superseded by the
   full `regbank` restoration passing with the real table intact).
6. **Unconstrained placement — the actual root cause.** Manuel's call,
   from watching Radiant's floorplan view: `spi1_rhd2164x2` had never had
   a physical location constraint and had drifted across builds. Fixed
   with `ldc_create_region`/`ldc_set_location` in `impl_1.pdc`, pinning
   `spi1_rhd2164x2` and (once restored) `controller0` to fixed regions.

Verified by putting the *entire* original design back, one block at a
time, with placement pinned throughout — PLL → `controller0` → `regbank`
→ `ch_sel` → `main_controller`/`fifo`/`dtx_mux_reg` → `spi0` — both chips
responding after every step. Final confirmation: real, distinct channel
data from both chips in the pc-app (channels 42 and 88 — chip0 and chip1
respectively, per the 2026-08-11 friendly-channel mapping), not just
digital SPI toggling.

New spec: `docs/interfaces/fpga-rhd2164-chip0-placement.md`. What's
established: pinning placement is necessary and sufficient across the
logic-content variation tested. What's not established: the exact
physical mechanism by which unpinned placement specifically broke chip0
and not chip1 — no routing-length comparison between bad/good placements
was done.

Noticed in passing during the floorplan work, tracked but deliberately
not acted on: **`regbank` is using a lot of FPGA area** — flagged for a
later reduction pass, deferred to avoid confounding this investigation.

## 3. Timing constraints, re-run on the placement-pinned design

Two STA runs, requested specifically as a before/after guideline pair:

- **§8 — baseline, only the stray 100 MHz `clkin` active**, on the
  placement-pinned, fully-restored design. 0 setup/hold errors. One
  wrinkle worth remembering: `clk`'s generated-clock ratio is fixed, so
  the wrong `clkin` dragged `clk`'s target to 139 MHz (3.15x too fast) —
  meaning this baseline's setup numbers were *stricter* than reality, and
  its hold numbers (a uniform +0.143 ns on the RHD2164 DDR receive paths)
  were the only ones that would carry forward unchanged, since hold
  doesn't scale with clock period.
- **§9 — full constraint set restored.** 0 setup/hold errors, both
  corners. §8's prediction held: the tightest setup path grew from
  +0.35 ns to +10.9–11.4 ns once the real 44.55 MHz period replaced the
  artificial one. MISO×2 and MOSI margins all *improved* over the
  pre-placement-fix numbers (§5.5) — plausibly tighter routing now that
  `spi1_rhd2164x2`/`controller0` have fixed locations. **CSB is the
  exception**: +1.563 ns → +1.462 ns, now under the 1.5 ns board/cable
  budget. Hold margins on the same DDR paths dropped from +0.143 ns to as
  low as 0.012 ns — expected, not a regression: `set_clock_uncertainty
  -setup -hold 0.125` is active in this run and wasn't in §8's baseline
  (0.143 − 0.125 = 0.018 ns, matching most of the reported slack almost
  exactly).

**Correction made to how the 1.5 ns number gets talked about**, per
Manuel: it was already flagged in the spec as an estimate, not a
measurement, but the CSB finding's first write-up implied a real number
had been crossed. Fixed throughout the spec and `PLAN.md`: the reason
real board/cable delay isn't measured is an **equipment ceiling** (needs
an oscilloscope sampling in the 10s of GSa/s, not a priority call), and
the `rx_a_en`/`rx_b_en`/`csb` pipelining fix buys **structural** margin
against PCB delay — moving SPI1's outputs off combinational logic onto a
direct register-to-pad path — that holds regardless of what the real,
unmeasured board delay turns out to be.
