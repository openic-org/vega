## 2. The frame could not say "not measured"

§6.2 as agreed had byte 1 as `reserved, must be 0`, and the two FPGA
fields as plain `uint32`/`uint16`.

That gives the FPGA counters two expressible states — zero, and non-zero
— for **three** real ones. The third is *"this build cannot read them"*,
which is not an edge case: it is what is true today, and stays true for
as long as A.7 step 1 is unbuilt.

Reporting it as `0` breaks the frame's own reason for existing. §7's
attribution table opens with "`fifo0_overflow_samples` moved → producer-
side loss"; with a silent zero, *did not move* and *cannot be read* are
the same bytes. And §6.3 already forbids exactly this shape one level up
— "a receiver must not infer that 'no telemetry frame yet' means 'no loss
yet'" — without noticing that the same trap sits inside the frame.

**Resolution:** byte 1 is now `flags`, bit 0 `fpga_counters_valid`. Same
frame size, every other offset unchanged, no `telemetry_version` bump
needed. When the bit is clear, both `fifo0_*` fields are transmitted as 0
and a receiver must not display or accumulate them — the pc-app shows
`n/a (no RTL counter)`, in grey, rather than a green zero.

This is the cheapest possible version of a fix that would have cost a
version bump if it had been found after the first frame shipped.

## 3. The regbank-read invariant §6.4 walked past

§6.4: *"The MCU reads them over the existing `REG_READ16` path that
A.1.1g already generalised — no new mechanism, just a counter and two
words."*

True about the primitive. But `fpga_spi.h` states the calling rule
explicitly:

> never interleaved with an in-progress `FPGA_SPI_ReadSamples()`.
> **Structural as built** — `0xFFF1` rejects these unless streaming is
> already stopped, and they execute from `StreamSendTask`'s stopped
> branch.

Every regbank access built so far — `SET_CHANNELS`, `REG_READ16`,
`REG_WRITE16`, `stream_enable` — obeys that by construction, and the
control-plane spec's whole §5 exists because an earlier inline placement
hung the MCU. A 1 Hz telemetry read while streaming is the first thing
that wants to violate it, and the agreed text does not mention that it
does.

**It is nonetheless safe, but only from one place, and that is worth
writing down rather than discovering.** The operative clause is
*interleaved with an in-progress read* — a bit-banged SPI0 sequence has
no preemption to interleave *within*, so what is really forbidden is
issuing from a context that can land mid-sequence: a BLE callback, an
ISR, or a state where a POP pair is left half-open. None apply at the top
of `StreamSendTask`'s send path: it is a packet boundary, A.1.1g's
trailing NOP already guarantees a clean FSM there (the same property
`StreamFlushFpgaFifo` relies on), and no command is in flight.

New §6.6 records this as three rules rather than a hope:

1. **Reads only, never writes.**
2. **From `StreamSendTask` only, at a packet boundary** — never from the
   `0xFFF1` callback, a timer, or the flow-off branch. The flow-off
   branch matters specifically: `fifo0` is under maximum pressure there,
   the worst possible moment to spend SPI0 time on anything but popping
   it.
3. **Skipped, not deferred, when the moment is wrong.** A stall that
   keeps the send loop away for a second costs that second's frame.
   Counters are cumulative, so the next frame carries everything the lost
   one would have.

Cost: three 2-transfer reads at ~10 µs/transfer ≈ **60 µs per second**,
0.006% of the send loop, three orders of magnitude inside `m` = 5.23%.
That is why the cadence is 1 Hz and not faster.

The read is not enabled in this build — `STREAM_TELEMETRY_FPGA_COUNTERS`
is `0` and the two word addresses are placeholders, because step 1's RTL
does not exist. Turning it on is that switch plus the addresses, and
nothing else in the chain changes, so the chain does not need re-testing
when step 1 lands.

## 4. §6.5 named a discard site that is not one

§6.5 listed two places where the MCU silently discards:

- the `flow_off:` path's clamped ring push, and
- `StreamContinuousPollDuringStall` (`:826-835`), "the same clamp shape".

Only the first is real. The second — and `StreamIngestDuringStall`, which
has the identical shape — clamps its FPGA **read**:

```c
uint32_t room = StreamRingFree();
uint32_t n = STREAM_PAIRS_PER_PACKET;
if (n > room) n = room;
FPGA_SPI_ReadSamples(tmp, n);   /* reads n, pushes n */
StreamRingPush(tmp, n);
```

A short read leaves the remainder in `fifo0`. That is the intended
backpressure, working — the thing §6.5's own closing paragraph says about
the rest of the headstage ("a full MCU ring stops `StreamIngestDuringStall`,
which pushes pressure back onto `fifo0` rather than dropping").

The `flow_off:` clamp looks the same and is not, because there the 59
pairs are **already out of the FIFO** when the notify fails; whatever
does not fit exists nowhere else. There is exactly one MCU-side discard
site, `ring_truncated_samples` counts it, and the spec now says so.

## 5. Two implementation choices worth recording

**Stall time is accumulated through a microsecond remainder, not by
dividing each stall.** At ~133 flow-offs/s, most resolving well under a
millisecond, `s_stall_time_ms_total += stall_us / 1000` truncates almost
every one to zero and reports a duty cycle of approximately nothing —
confidently, and in the one number step 3b exists to measure. Carrying a
`s_stall_us_remainder` costs one `uint32` and is exact.

**Loss is computed by differencing consecutive frames, not by comparing
totals.** §6.7 (new) records why this is worth doing under v0 at all: the
pc-app can count the same aggregate the MCU's `anchor_sample_index`
counts (2 per pair), and the shortfall between them over an interval
**is** the whole-path sample loss — available now, without the v1 header,
and independent of `seq_num`, which counts only packets and cannot see a
short packet or a truncated frame.

Differencing rather than absolute comparison is what makes it robust: a
counter reset on the far end (`START_STREAMING`, a reconnect) appears as
an implausible delta and the interval is skipped, so **no reset has to be
tracked on the pc-app side at all.** Tested, including the reset case.

## 7. Bench: chip0 is intermittent, and three root causes fall with it

The session's second half was a bench report from Manuel that turned into
**the most consequential finding of the day**, and it is not the
telemetry work.

Full write-up and the work it generates: **PLAN.md A.1.2**, now item 0 on
the critical path. Recorded here is what was observed, and — more usefully
— how the reasoning went wrong twice before it went right.

### 7.1 The observation

1. Loaded the previously-committed bitstream (`324a21c`, SHA-256
   `7a5418d6…`). chip0: **all `0xFFFF`**, the signature Manuel recognises
   as the SCK/MOSI timing failure. chip1 fine.
2. Rebuilt and reflashed. Same, minutes later.
3. System left powered and untouched for several hours. On return, **both
   chips responding** — no power cycle, no reconfiguration, no command.
4. Manuel: not new. A bitstream that had been working has failed on a
   later reload before, on occasions spanning **days and weeks**.

### 7.2 Three readings, two of them wrong

Worth recording in order, because each was a reasonable inference from
what was known at the time and each was overturned by one more fact.

**Reading 1 — "`324a21c`'s bitstream is bad."** Manuel opened with "the
same bitstream is producing different responses as last time." Hashing the
committed artifacts showed three distinct bitstreams and a fourth in the
working tree, and that the one he had loaded was `324a21c`'s — whose
re-confirmation PLAN.md had been carrying as *outstanding* since 08-31,
while its own commit message claimed "Bench-verified: both chips
responding." So the premise looked false: this was not the same bitstream
behaving differently, it was the outstanding re-confirmation failing.
Clean, and wrong.

**Reading 2 — "marginal, latched at boot."** Manuel: *"this is not the
first time that I re-load the bitstream that was working the night before
and produces a different behaviour."* That reframed it from a regression
to marginality — and with it came the observation that **on a marginal
system, a single pass is not evidence of a fix.** Combined with "always
all-or-nothing per boot," this pointed at something latched during
initialisation, and produced a concrete proposal: detect chip0 at boot and
report loudly.

**Reading 3 — the actual one.** *"I was on meetings and left the system
on… now both chips are responding."* Recovery with no intervention refutes
"latched at boot" outright. The only variable that changes over hours of
idle powered operation is **temperature**.

### 7.3 What the finding actually is

Established by (1)–(3) alone, independent of any recollection: the failure
is **not deterministic in the bitstream**, **not latched at
initialisation**, and **drifts over hours**.

Temperature is the hypothesis — well-supported, not measured. There are no
logged temperatures. A tidy "evening warm / morning cold" story appeared
briefly in the discussion and was **withdrawn**: it was built out of the
phrase "last night", which Manuel then clarified was a figure of speech
for occasions days and weeks back, not a literal previous evening. The
direction (warm works, cold fails) rests on the untouched recovery plus
Manuel's own independent impression about cold and warm days.

The clarification *strengthened* the conclusion rather than weakening it:
a phenomenon spanning weeks was present before, during and after all three
chip0 "root causes" — exactly what would be expected if none of them
touched it.

### 7.4 The systematic bias, which is the real damage

chip0 has been root-caused three times — unconstrained placement
(08-24), the SCK/MOSI regression (08-31), and this session's initial
suspicion of `324a21c`. **Every one was confirmed by a single pass.**

On a system intermittent over hours that is not evidence, and the loop had
a bias toward false confirmation baked in: change RTL on a cold board in
the morning → observe failure → debug for hours while the board warms →
observe the fix working. That manufactures a convincing causal story for
whatever was changed that day, every day.

It also explains a contradiction this session spent time treating as a
bookkeeping error: `324a21c`'s "Bench-verified: both chips responding" and
PLAN.md's "re-confirmation outstanding" were **never in conflict.** Two
honest reports of two different boots. The records were trying to say
something and were read as sloppy.

`docs/interfaces/fpga-rhd2164-chip0-placement.md` and
`fpga-timing-constraints.md` now carry an UNDER REVIEW banner saying so.

### 7.5 A claim made earlier today, corrected

Having found that SCK = `clk`/2 exactly (the SPI controller is a
hand-unrolled FSM, two `clk` states per SCK half-period, no divider), it
was argued here that λ = 28,000's `clk` = 42.504 MHz would **help** chip0
by relaxing SCK from 22.770 to 21.252 MHz — and that this inverted the
plan's treatment of the retune from risk to fix.

**Probably wrong, if the temperature direction holds.** Colder silicon is
faster, so a failure appearing as delays *shorten* is a **hold**
violation, and hold violations are frequency-independent — stretching the
period does not change how long data stays valid after an edge. If it is
hold, 42.504 MHz will not help and neither would 20 MHz.

It also suggests 08-31's four-phase deskew sweep may have had the right
signal and the wrong sign: the remedy for hold is *more* delay on MOSI
relative to SCK, not less.

Left in PLAN.md as an explicit caution, because the retune is going to be
flashed anyway and a chip0 pass afterwards must not be read as the retune
having helped.

### 7.6 Why measurement now outranks fixing

The work A.1.2 generates is deliberately all measurement, and none of it
needs the 10s-of-GSa/s scope that blocked the 2026-08-24 attempt:
reproduce on demand with freeze spray (which converts an intermittent
ghost into a debuggable fault — the thing every previous attempt lacked),
halve `clk` while cold to separate hold from setup in one PLL change, then
pass rate versus logged temperature.

And the reason it is item 0 rather than item 3: **until the pass rate is
known, no bench result on this board can be scored.** That includes the
telemetry bring-up built earlier today. A change that appears to fix
chip0, or appears not to break it, is indistinguishable from a warm board.

### 7.7 Consequences

- **Animal test.** A cold start in a surgical suite can yield a one-shot
  recording with one channel of two, silently. A warm-up-and-verify step
  before the subject is anaesthetised is **mandatory** until this closes,
  and belongs in A.0's checklist rather than in anyone's memory.
- **Pre-session self-test** (B.5) is promoted, and its shape changes: it
  must be **re-runnable at session start**, not boot-only, because the
  state moves while powered.
- **λ = 28,000 survives.** μ and λ are transport measurements and do not
  depend on which RHD channels are live. Results that required *both*
  chips responding are the ones to re-check.
