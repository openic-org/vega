## 3. The chip0 regression, and a day of FPGA constraint archaeology

Reloading the (then still-uncommitted) bitstream from the previous session
found chip0 unresponsive again — same signature as the 2026-08-24/27
regressions. Chased it through several stages before landing on the real
cause:

- Found `impl_1.sdc` had every real timing constraint commented out except
  a stray `clkin @ 100 MHz` line. Read as an accidental regression;
  Manuel disagreed and said it was intentional — over-constraining the
  clock (declaring `clkin` faster than real) forces PAR to place things
  tighter, and that alone made chip0 respond again.
- Proposed replacing the period-scaling with a real constraint set plus a
  `set_clock_uncertainty` margin (`CLK_SETUP_MARGIN_NS`) instead, to avoid
  corrupting `spi1_csb`'s external budget the way period-scaling did
  (`spi1_sck`'s declared period shrinks along with `clk`'s, and `spi1_csb`'s
  fixed 21.5 ns external budget no longer fits inside it — an
  arithmetically-guaranteed failure that Radiant reports under a "Clock
  Domain Crossing" heading, which is not what it actually is). Verified
  via a fresh `.twr` — but used the wrong `clk` frequency (44.55 MHz
  instead of the real, 2026-08-27-retuned 45.54 MHz) in the process; Manuel
  caught and fixed it before it went anywhere.
- Manuel identified the actual root cause by inspection: `spi1_sck`/
  `spi1_mosi`/`spi1_csb` are a single FPGA pad each, fanning out on the
  board to *both* RHD2164 chips — a shared, multi-drop bus. If the
  physical trace length from the FPGA to chip0 differs from the trace to
  chip1, one chip's setup/hold margin is worse than the other's, and
  nothing FPGA-internal can differentially compensate a shared signal.
- First fix attempt: a new placement region (`region0`/`group0`) to
  deliberately deskew MOSI, requiring removal of the whole-design
  placement pinning (`mregion0`-`mregion7`). This regressed further — the
  diagnostics ladder went flat/wrong (`0x0004` regardless of slot or
  chip — the FPGA's default `READ(63)` chip-ID answer, not what was
  actually injected) on every rung, and the plain, non-diagnostic baseline
  signal degraded to "weird things" too.

Given the compounding regression, decided to revert both repos to the last

## 4. Register console corruption — a real, separate firmware bug

Re-running the ladder on the reverted checkpoint, rung `L` passed its two
real observations (`0x0035`/`0x003A` exact, both chips) but **aborted
during restore**: a write-verification mismatch, regbank holding `0xCC00`
where `0x3F00` (`CONVERT(63)`) was expected. A second run aborted the same
way, this time during *setup*: `0xCCFB` where `0xFB00` (`READ(59)`) was
expected. The repeating `0xCC` — the first byte of `CMD_MAGIC` — was the
tell: a magic byte leaking into a write's staged high byte, not an
FPGA/RTL problem.

Traced to a real gap in `wb09ke-bridge`: USART1's RX overrun (ORE) at
2 Mbaud, already known since 2026-08-06, silently drops the byte that
triggered it. The existing fix only cleared the ORE flag so RX didn't die
*permanently* — it never told `VEGA_UART_RxByte()`'s command-frame parser
that a byte was lost mid-frame, so an ORE mid-payload leaves the parser
misaligned with no way to detect it, and the *next* frame's `0xCC 0x33`
magic gets consumed as payload data instead of recognized as a new frame.

Fixed: `VEGA_UART_RxReset()` resets the parser to `RX_IDLE`, called from
the ORE handler right after clearing the overrun; plus a 5 ms RX timeout
backstop inside `VEGA_UART_RxByte()` itself for any other desync source
(2 Mbaud byte gap ~5 µs, so this never trips on real traffic; ~1000x
shorter than the pc-app's ~2000 ms retry gap, so a retry now always finds
the parser at `RX_IDLE`). Bench-verified: rung `L` and Get Settings (which
had separately hung twice, needing a bridge reset) both re-run afterward
with multiple retries firing on the still-lossy transport, all recovering
cleanly. Doesn't reduce how often USART1 drops a byte — only how cleanly
recovery happens when it does. Committed `wb09ke-bridge` `0836caf`, merged
to `vega` `main`.

With the fix in, re-ran the full ladder clean (all seven rungs, §1) and
confirmed Get Settings working ("7 registers read").

## 6. Second SCK/MOSI attempt — a phase-shifted-clock deskew, verified by STA, still didn't work

With the checkpoint solid, went back to the chip0/SCK-MOSI regression with
a cleaner approach: rather than fighting placement, use the PLL itself.
Added `clk90` (a second PLL output, `CLKOS`, phase-shifted from `clk`) and
planned to re-launch `spi1_mosi` off it — deliberately deskewing MOSI from
`spi1_sck` (itself newly registered, `sck_reg`, mirroring `csb_reg`'s
existing 2026-08-24 pattern) to compensate for the RHD2164 pair's
board-level trace-length asymmetry.

Sorted out the actual PLL config with real numbers rather than guessing:
initial ballpark was "45° ≈ 6 ns," checked against the regenerated
`pll0.ldc` and corrected — 45° of the retuned 45.539955 MHz period is
2.74 ns, and ~6 ns is actually closer to 90° (5.49 ns) — Manuel confirmed
90° was what he'd meant and regenerated the PLL accordingly. Wrote the
`.sdc` side (`clk90` as its own `create_clock` with the real waveform
pulled from `pll0.ldc`, not derived by hand) while Manuel wrote the RTL
(`sck_reg`, `mosi_reg`, the `clk90` plumbing through `spi_master_rhd2164x2.v`
and `kuntur_fpga.v`).

**STA fully verified the structural change**: 0 negative slack all 3
corners, 98.2% coverage — matching the historical clean baseline exactly.
`spi1_mosi`'s own margin came out to 4.939 ns (path trace confirmed
`Source Clock: clk90` → `Destination Clock: spi1_sck`, real numbers, not
assumed) — meaningfully *better* than before, proving the phase direction
wasn't actively harmful the way a wrongly-signed shift would have been.
One flagged, confirmed side effect: `spi1_csb` tightened from 1.060 ns to
0.417 ns (still passing) once `sck_reg` was added, exactly the kind of
FSM-resequencing effect flagged before the RTL was written — CSB and SCK
now carry the same one-cycle latency relative to `current_state` that they
didn't before, when only CSB had it.

**Didn't work on real hardware anyway.** Flashed it; chip0 stayed
unresponsive. Tried three more `mosi_reg` clock relationships (`clk`,
`negedge clk`, `negedge clk90`) — none got both chips responding. Root
cause of the miss, per Manuel: 90° was picked from a rough ns estimate of
the needed deskew, never a measurement of the real board asymmetry — STA
proving the internal timing is clean says nothing about whether 90° is the
right *amount* of compensation for an unmeasured physical skew.

Isolated the failure with a clean control: reclocked `mosi_reg` onto plain
`clk` (same net as `sck_reg`, not just nominal same-phase) — also didn't
work, meaning the register-adding itself, independent of any deskew
amount, breaks something. Given time, closed this for now rather than
keep iterating blind: reverted `sck_reg`/`mosi_reg` to plain passthrough
assigns (`assign sck_reg = sck; assign mosi_reg = mosi;`) — functionally
identical to the original combinational design — while keeping `clk90`,
the PLL routing, and the register declarations in place (commented out
where they'd conflict), so a future attempt doesn't have to redo the
PLL/RTL plumbing from scratch. Bench-confirmed both chips responding again
on this exact build.

**Real bonus in this final state**: it carries the 2026-08-27 PLL retune
(`clk` = 21.9587393093 ns / 45.539955 MHz, exact 30,000 SPS) — verified on
hardware that day but never actually committed to git until now. Three
sessions of drift closed in the same commit that closes the SCK/MOSI
question for today. Committed `kuntur` `324a21c` on
`session-2026-08-31-checkpoint`, pushed.
