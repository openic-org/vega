## 1. Closing out the chronic data-loss investigation

### 1.1 Category B: `BLE_STACK_Tick()` blocking ~10–22 ms after flow-off resume

Oscilloscope work earlier in the investigation (PA10 as a flow-off/resume
marker at the time, PB1 task heartbeat, a repurposed debug GPIO bracketing
`BLE_STACK_Tick()`) isolated the mechanism directly: `BLE_STACK_Tick()`
blocks the *entire* cooperative scheduler for ~10–22 ms immediately after
every BLE TX flow-off resume — not just skipping the SPI read, the whole
scheduler goes silent for that window. `StreamSendTask` resumes ~83–85 µs
after `BLE_STACK_Tick()` returns, confirming a direct causal chain rather
than "scheduler skipping our task."

Deepening the FPGA's hardware FIFO (1024→4096 pairs, `kuntur_fpga.v`,
`ADDR_WIDTH` 10→12) did **not** fix the data loss — it only delayed it: a
100 s recording showed `fifo_full` asserting once and never clearing again
for the rest of the recording. Measured directly from the CSV: production
rate (FPGA ramp-counter advance, ground truth) stayed flat at
~29,350–29,390 SPS throughout, while delivered rate dropped from 29,344
(healthy) to 28,844 SPS (stuck) — a **chronic average-throughput deficit**,
not a worst-case-blackout-length problem. Root structural cause: the
MCU's steady-state packet cycle was tuned to hit ~30,000 SPS with **zero
built-in surplus margin** — any FIFO depth only changes how long it takes
to saturate, not whether it does.

**The fix**: since steady-state has ~0 surplus and each flow-off costs a
full, unavoidable ~10–22 ms blackout, the only lever left (without touching
acquisition mode/cadence) is reducing how *often* that tax is paid.
`CFG_BLE_MBLOCK_COUNT_MARGIN` in `kuntur-mcu/Core/Inc/app_conf.h`:
`54 → 200`.

| Metric | Before (margin=54) | After (margin=200) |
|---|---|---|
| Flow-off rate | 0.842/s | ~0.0127/s (~66× fewer) |
| Production vs. delivered deficit | chronic, accumulating (locked into ~1.86%/s loss once FIFO saturated) | +0.42–0.45 sps (0.0015%), flat across every 60 s bucket — zero drift |
| `fifo_full` | asserted, never cleared | never asserted the entire recording |
| Bench throughput | 500.2 pps, std 5.0 | 506.0 pps, std 1.5 |

Confirmed on two independent clean recordings (315.6 s and 338.1 s, no
MCU/bridge resets) — deficit agreed to the decimal point between runs. At
the residual +0.43 sps rate, the 4096-pair FIFO would take 2.5+ hours to
fill — a non-issue in practice. The underlying `BLE_STACK_Tick()` block
itself is still unexplained (an ST support ticket is planned, not yet
filed), but flow-offs now happen rarely enough that the system runs with a
small positive throughput margin instead of a slow chronic deficit.

### 1.2 Diagnostic-tooling fixes that enabled confirming the fix

Two bugs in the analysis tooling had been producing misleading SKP counts
that muddied the picture while chasing the mblock fix above:

- **`analyze_recording.py` OOM crash on multi-hour recordings** — fixed to
  allow running the full-length soak-test recordings needed to actually see
  the chronic (slow-accumulating) deficit rather than just short samples.
- **ch0 wraparound bug** — the analyzer didn't unwrap the ch0 16-bit ramp's
  rollover, misreading legitimate wraps as huge bogus SKPs. Fixed with a
  modular unwrap; also added a `correlate_stalls` tool.
- **"Category A" phantom SKPs — the big one.** A separate, long-running
  mystery: 46–48 SKP-like events per run (scaling to exactly 1,679 on an
  hour-long soak test), magnitude always exactly +2, at a strikingly
  regular ~2.2–2.25 s period matching the ch0 16-bit ramp's own rollover
  period. Originally suspected FPGA/hardware (carry-chain timing at
  rollover), chased across multiple sessions including static RTL tracing
  and synthesis timing reports (0 negative slack, no support for the
  hardware theory).

  **Actual root cause, found by inspecting the raw CSV instead of the
  RTL**: `analyze_recording.py`, `packet_parser.py`'s `fifo_underruns`
  count, and `graph_widget.py`'s live-plot filter all classified a sample
  as a "FIFO underrun" whenever `ch0 == -32768` *alone*. But the ramp test
  pattern's own value legitimately equals -32768 once per 16-bit wrap —
  and at that exact sample, `ch1 = ch0 + 1000 = -31768`, not -32768. A
  *true* underrun always sets **both** channels to `0x8000`. Checking ch0
  only silently deleted one real, valid sample per wrap right before the
  step-continuity check ever ran — exactly producing the phantom "+2 SKP"
  pattern. Cross-tabulating `ch1` at every `ch0==-32768` row confirmed it
  precisely: 51,237 real underruns (`ch1==-32768`) vs. exactly 1,679
  misclassified real samples (`ch1==-31768`) in a 3M-row slice, scaling to
  1,679/1,679 over the full file — a perfect match to the SKP count.

  **Fix**: all three sentinel checks changed from `ch0 == -32768` to
  `(ch0 == -32768) & (ch1 == -32768)`. Re-running the analyzer on the same
  hour-long recording: **SKP count 0/0 (was 1,679), ch0 step continuity
  100.0%**.

  Also fixed earlier: `CsvRecorder` was silently dropping underrun rows,
  which made the reported "underruns: 0%" stat always a lie regardless of
  the real rate.

Together, these tooling fixes were what let the mblock margin fix above
actually be *verified* — before they landed, a genuine hardware/software
fix (margin=200) would have still shown a nonzero, "hardware-looking"
periodic SKP count from the analysis side alone, masking the real result.

## 2. Starting state: MCU unresponsive after an accidental terminal close

The previous session's terminal was closed by mistake mid-soak-test. On
resuming, the Kuntur MCU (freshly flashed) produced **zero UART output and no
BLE data** — matching a "power-cycle needed after flash" mystery already
flagged as unresolved in that prior session's notes. Initial checks:

- `/dev/ttyACM0`/`ttyACM1` (both ST-LINK-V3 probes) present but hadn't
  re-enumerated in hours — board hadn't been unplugged, just wedged.
- `tio` UART captures: 0 bytes, no boot banner at all.
- pc-app connection attempts: brief connect then 0 kbps/0 pps, or nothing.

## 3. SWD investigation — finding where the CPU was actually stuck

Attached via `STM32_Programmer_CLI` to the Kuntur-side ST-LINK-V3
(SN `002300373233510739363634`, distinct from the WB09KE bridge's probe).
Plain connects failed consistently (`Unable to get core ID`) — turned out to
be contention with the STM32CubeProgrammer **GUI** still running in the
background (`STM32CubeProgrammerLauncher` process); closing it let a bare
connect succeed.

Getting a *reliable* connect still required timing: pressing the physical
reset button and immediately retrying a connect in a tight loop (matching the
user's own known-working manual procedure — press reset, then connect within
the firmware's existing 5-second debugger-attach window). Once connected and
halted:

- **`PC = 0x100017D0`**, reproducible across independent reset+halt captures
  — inside the STM32WB09's internal 6 KB boot-ROM region (`0x10000000`–
  `0x100017FF` per the linker script's `REGION_ROM`), never in the
  application. Not a HardFault (XPSR IPSR=0, Thread mode).
- Application vector table at `0x10040000` looked structurally valid
  (SP=`0x20010000`, Reset_Handler=`0x100466F4`) — ruled out a corrupted
  flash image.
- `FLASH->CONFIG` = `0x00000010` → `REMAP` bit clear — address 0 still
  mirrored flash, meaning the boot ROM had never made its "enter
  application" decision.
- `PWR->SR2` = `0x0000C3B6` → `IOBOOTVAL[3:0]` = `0xC` = PA11=1, **PA10=1**,
  PA9=0, PA8=0.

## 4. Root cause: PA10 is a boot-strap pin

STM32WB09xE datasheet (DS14210 §3.22, "Embedded UART bootloader"):

> "Bootloader is activated by hardware by forcing PA10 high during hardware
> reset, otherwise, application residing in flash is launched."

PA10 had been repurposed on 2026-07-31 (a prior session) from an
MCU-driven output (an old scope-trigger marker — the same PA10 signal used
in §1.1's oscilloscope work above) into an MCU **input** wired directly to
the FPGA's `fifo_full` signal (`GPIO_NOPULL`, "actively driven by the
FPGA"), for a diagnostic in `stream_app.c` (`StreamFifoFullWatchPoll()`,
gated by `STREAM_DIAG_FIFO_FULL_WATCH`). Whatever level the FPGA happened
to drive on that net at MCU power-on now determined whether the
application ever launched — and since `fifo_full` is known to chatter
continuously (~30–60 µs edges, per §1.1), it likely kept the embedded
bootloader's own auto-baud-detect wait from ever timing out, producing a
true permanent hang rather than just an occasional bad sample.

## 5. Fix applied

`STM32_BLE/App/stream_app.c`: `STREAM_DIAG_FIFO_FULL_WATCH` flipped `1→0`,
compiling out the GPIO init, the poll function, and its `StreamSendTask`
call site — PA10 is no longer touched by firmware at all. User also
physically disconnected the ad-hoc FPGA-to-PA10 bodge wire and power-cycled
the board.

Verified no other PA10/`GPIO_PIN_10` references remain anywhere in the
firmware (all other hits were comments or an EXTI handler already removed
earlier the same day).

Rebuilt clean (headless STM32CubeIDE build), reflashed, **confirmed working
via live streaming data in the pc-app** across multiple subsequent
reflashes.

## 6. Internal pull-down attempt — tried, verified ineffective, reverted

Explored fixing the residual ~5 second boot delay (the bootloader's
auto-baud timeout, no longer a hang but not instant either) by configuring
an internal pull-down via `HAL_PWREx_EnableGPIOPullDown(PWR_GPIO_A,
PWR_GPIO_BIT_10)` early in `main()`.

- Found via RM0505 §5.7.8 that `PWRC_PUCRA` (not the normal GPIO `PUPDR`)
  has an **internal pull-up on PA10 enabled by default at reset**
  (`0x0000 0F07`) — this, not a floating pin, is the actual reason PA10
  reads high with nothing externally attached. (An earlier schematic trace
  claiming PA10 was wired to test point TP5 was independently checked by
  the user and found wrong — TP5 is actually PB1.)
- Verified empirically via SWD, after a confirmed-successful boot, that
  `PWR->SR2` still read `IOBOOTVAL` bit2=1 on the very next reset —
  `PWRC_PDCRA` resets to its own default at the same reset event that
  triggers the `IOBOOTVAL` sample, so firmware can never win this race on
  any reset, warm or cold.
- **Reverted** the change; rebuilds clean without it.
- Side discovery: `STM32_Programmer_CLI`'s SWD "Normal mode" connect on
  this board appears to force a reset internally as part of its own
  connection recovery, rather than passively catching a user-triggered one
  — confirmed once via a visible glitch in the pc-app plot coinciding with
  an SWD check performed while the app was already running fine.

## 7. Re-applied the `StreamDataPacket_t` alignment fix

A separate, previously-known issue: `StreamDataPacket_t` (`stream.h`) is
`__attribute__((packed))`, which in GCC drops the whole struct's alignment
to 1 byte — risky on Cortex-M0/M0+ (no hardware unaligned-access support;
an unaligned access silently HardFaults into a `while(1)`). This fix
(`packed, aligned(4)`) had been tried earlier in the investigation and
**reverted** after a bisection seemed to show the "MCU silent" symptom
persisting even with it applied.

With PA10 now understood as the actual root cause of that symptom, the
earlier bisection was recognized as confounded. Re-applied
`__attribute__((packed, aligned(4)))`; rebuild confirmed both
`-Waddress-of-packed-member` warnings gone (18 → 16 total warnings, 0
errors). Reflashed, confirmed working via live pc-app data.
